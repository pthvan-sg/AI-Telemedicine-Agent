# Migraine and headache

Demo corpus summarised from widely published general knowledge. Not clinical guidance.

## Red flags for secondary headache
Warning features include sudden severe (thunderclap) onset, a new neurological deficit such as weakness, vision loss or confusion, fever with neck stiffness, headache after head trauma, new headache after age 50, headache that worsens with exertion or coughing, and headache in someone with cancer or immunosuppression. These patients need urgent assessment.

## Triptan cautions
Triptans are contraindicated in ischemic heart disease, uncontrolled hypertension, and hemiplegic or basilar migraine. They must not be combined with MAO inhibitors or ergot derivatives. Taking them together with SSRIs or SNRIs carries a rare risk of serotonin syndrome, so counsel the patient on symptoms.

## Medication overuse headache
Frequent use of simple analgesics (about 15 or more days a month) or triptans (about 10 or more days a month) can cause medication overuse headache. A headache diary helps identify triggers and the frequency of analgesic use.

## Lifestyle measures
Regular sleep, regular meals, hydration, and limiting caffeine and known triggers are commonly advised alongside acute and preventive treatment.
