# Antibiotics and drug allergy

Demo corpus summarised from widely published general knowledge. Not clinical guidance.

## Penicillin allergy
Patients with a documented penicillin allergy should not receive penicillin-class antibiotics, which include amoxicillin, ampicillin and amoxicillin-clavulanate. Cross-reactivity with cephalosporins is low but not zero, so the reaction history matters. Many reported penicillin allergies are not true allergies, and formal allergy testing can clarify this.

## Sulfonamide allergy
Patients with a sulfonamide antibiotic allergy should avoid sulfamethoxazole and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole.

## Acute sinusitis
Most acute sinusitis is viral and settles within about 10 days. Antibiotics are usually reserved for symptoms lasting beyond 10 days, severe features such as high fever with purulent discharge, or double worsening.

## Always document allergy status
Allergy status should be recorded before any prescription is written. If it was not discussed, the note should say it is not documented rather than assume there is none.
